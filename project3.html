Case Study 04

Humming Salad Bar

A mobile web app where you hum a tune into your microphone and it generates a personalized salad recipe. The brief was to design a unique interaction using sound, with no words allowed.

Role Interaction Design, Full Stack
Timeline Feb to Mar 2021
Context CPSC 581 · HCI II
Tools HTML/CSS/JS, Bootstrap, Illustrator

Problem Statement

For my Human Computer Interaction II course at the University of Calgary, the problem was to design a novel interaction where sound was the primary input mechanism. The catch was that the sound couldn't be words. No speech recognition, no commands, no language at all. The challenge was to take something as raw and informal as a hum, a tap, a whistle, and turn it into a meaningful interaction that a person could actually understand and respond to.

I explored a few directions early on, including a cat game controlled by meowing and a rhythm based cricket game, before landing on the one that felt the most genuinely surprising: a salad bar that listens to you hum. It's a bit absurd, and that's exactly why it works.

The Concept

You open the app, set your dietary preferences, and then hum into your microphone. Based on the characteristics of the hum, pitch, duration and pattern, the app generates a personalized salad bowl recipe. The same song hummed at a different pitch produces a completely different salad, which means the interaction rewards curiosity and repeat plays in a way that a conventional input would never achieve.

The delight is partly in the absurdity and partly in the fact that it actually works. You feel like your input is doing something real, even if the logic connecting humming to salad ingredients is intentionally playful.

Final Design

I went with a light green palette and hand drawn Illustrator assets throughout. The visual identity was meant to communicate the tone of the app before anyone read a single word: this is playful, this is a bit weird, and that's intentional. If the design felt too polished or too serious it would have undercut the whole premise.

What I Took Away

The biggest tension in this project was between randomness and meaning. Too much randomness and the app feels like a novelty with no reason to come back to. Too little, and the connection between humming and salad feels completely arbitrary. I spent a fair amount of time thinking about how to make the relationship between input and output feel legible without making it feel mechanical, because the moment it becomes predictable, the surprise is gone.

The broader thing I took away was how much a constraint can open things up. The no words rule pushed me into territory I wouldn't have gone near with an open brief, and the result was far more interesting than any conventional recipe generator I could have designed. I think about that pretty often when a brief feels restrictive.
